Why New Lines Fail

Most failed launches share a few common causes:

The owner falls in love with the idea and skips the evidence.
The product is built for an imagined customer instead of a real one.
The price is set by cost rather than by what buyers will pay.
The launch depends on a big marketing push that never quite delivers.

Underneath all of these is the same issue: decisions made on assumptions. Validation replaces assumptions with evidence, one step at a time.

Step One: Write Down Your Assumptions

Before testing anything, list what must be true for your new line to succeed. Be specific.

For example:

Mid-sized companies in Dubai need this service at least once a quarter.
They currently use a provider they are not happy with.
They will pay at least a certain amount per month.
We can reach them through search and professional networks.
We can deliver at a margin of at least thirty percent.

Each line is a hypothesis. Some will turn out to be right, some wrong. The goal is to find out which, cheaply and quickly.

Rank them by risk. The assumptions that would kill the idea if they were wrong should be tested first.

Step Two: Talk to Real Buyers

Nothing replaces direct conversations with potential customers. Aim for fifteen to twenty short interviews with people who match your target profile.

Ask About the Problem, Not Your Idea

Do not start by pitching. Ask about their current situation, what frustrates them, how they solve the problem today, and what it costs them.

Listen for strong emotion and real spending. A problem people already pay to solve is a far better sign than one they simply agree exists.

Look for Patterns

After ten or so conversations, patterns appear.

You may find that:

The problem is real but affects a different type of customer.
Buyers care about speed more than price.
Buyers prioritise price over speed.

Adjust your idea based on what you hear.

Use Your Existing Customers

If you already have customers, they are the easiest people to speak with.

Ask whether they would buy the new line, what they would expect from it, and what they would pay. Existing trust makes these conversations more honest.
